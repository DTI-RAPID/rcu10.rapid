_More than a place to enjoy coffee, MC² was built around Jim Kaiser Caintoy's vision of creating a “third place” where people could connect and feel a sense of belonging. His journey reflects how starting small and building meaningful relationships can open doors to greater opportunities._

In 2022, Jim Kaiser Caintoy began selling cold brew made from 100% Arabica coffee at the Provincial Capitol grounds in Malaybalay City, Bukidnon. With basic brewing equipment, limited resources, and support from his younger sibling and family, he took his first step toward building a business of his own.

His first customers were mostly friends and curious passersby. As more people discovered his coffee, some became regular customers, giving him the confidence to continue.

Today, MC² has grown from a weekend stand into a café offering around 30 products and providing employment to 15 full-time and part-time team members. What began as a small business has gradually become the welcoming space Jim had envisioned.

**From Coffee Professional to Business Owner**

Jim's journey in the coffee industry began when he joined a multinational coffeehouse chain in Davao City. Over eight years, he progressed from a part-time employee to a coffee master, supervisor, and eventually a manager.

During his time with the company, he learned that coffee beans sourced from Mirayon, Bukidnon, were being used in one of its specialty coffee blends. For Jim, knowing that coffee from his home province had reached an international coffee company was a source of pride and inspiration.

“It felt special that a big company was getting coffee from Bukidnon,” he recalled. “I was very inspired.”

In 2018, Jim resigned and returned to Bukidnon to spend more time with his child and explore opportunities outside corporate employment. However, adjusting to life without a regular income proved difficult.

“I thought it would be easy to have no job,” he shared. “It wasn't.”

During the pandemic, he explored different livelihoods, including selling fish, siomai, and ice cream. These experiences taught him to manage limited resources, understand customers, and recognize opportunities.

“We are not born rich,” he said. “I wanted the status of my life not to stay the same.”

Determined to improve his circumstances, Jim returned to his passion for coffee.

**Starting Small, Building Connections**

Together with his younger sibling, Jim pooled available resources and purchased basic equipment to begin selling three cold brew beverages on weekends.

Drawing from his experience in coffee preparation and customer service, he focused not only on product quality but also on building relationships with customers.

“I am grateful that they trusted my product,” he shared. “Our goal is to make them come back.”

As demand increased, the siblings began operating daily and eventually rented a small space along Luminton Street in Malaybalay City, Bukidnon. Participation in the Kaamulan Festival and connections with fellow business owners further introduced MC²  to new customers.

Through continued reinvestment and customer support, they eventually moved into a larger space along Pimentel Street in Malaybalay City, Bukidnon.

“It is really a slow process,” Jim reflected.

Along the way, some of his earliest customers became employees, business partners, or business owners themselves. These relationships reflected his vision of creating a place where connections could grow into opportunities beyond sharing a cup of coffee.

**Adding Value to Local Coffee, Creating Opportunities for Farmers**

As MC² expanded, securing a reliable supply of quality coffee beans became a major challenge. Despite operating in Bukidnon, Jim initially struggled to find suppliers willing to accommodate his small orders. There were even days before when the shop could not open because coffee beans were unavailable.

A turning point came when he connected with the Bayanihan Millennium Multipurpose Cooperative (BMMPC), a coffee-producing cooperative in Pangantucan, Bukidnon.

“Even though I was new to business, they trusted me,” he recalled.

Their partnership began with purchases of around 20–30 kilograms of coffee beans, eventually increasing to approximately 100–140 kilograms as demand grew.

What started as a solution to his supply challenges became an opportunity to support local farmers. By purchasing their coffee and bringing it closer to consumers, Jim recognized how his business could contribute to the local coffee value chain.

Rather than becoming a coffee farmer himself, he saw greater potential in applying his experience to roasting, processing, and marketing locally produced beans. Through these activities, he hopes to add value to farmers' products, strengthen market linkages, and create more opportunities for Bukidnon coffee to reach consumers.

To pursue this direction, Jim sought assistance from government agencies. Through the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Department of Science and Technology (DOST), and Small Business Corporation (SB Corp), he accessed business development assistance, training, trade promotions, and financing opportunities.

MC² is among the MSMEs supported by the DTI-RAPID Growth Project under the Bukidnon Expanded Arabica Coffee Development Investment Plan (DIP). Through this initiative, the project provides assistance in capacity building, market access, and productive investment to strengthen businesses and their linkages with local coffee farmers.

One of the project's interventions is facilitating a Commercial Partnership Agreement (CPA) between MC² and BMMPC. The agreement helps formalize their business relationship, providing farmers with a more stable market for their coffee while helping MC² secure a sustained supply of quality beans.

MC² is also pursuing a matching grant for a 5-kilogram coffee roaster and coffee destoner, with ₱640,000 proposed for funding by the project, representing 40% of the investment, while MC² provides the remaining 60%. MC² and project team are currently completing the necessary documentary requirements and processes, subject to approval.

Once realized, the equipment is expected to improve MC²'s processing capacity and product consistency, allowing Jim to add greater value to locally sourced coffee and strengthen his role in connecting farmers' produce with consumers.

“I think with that path, I can help the farmers more,” he shared.

For Jim, growing MC² also means creating opportunities for the farmers whose coffee makes that growth possible.

**More Than Coffee: A Third Place for the Community**

For Jim, MC² was never meant to be just another café. He envisioned a welcoming space where people could gather, relax, and feel connected.

“MC² is here to provide you a third place,” he explained.

He describes home as the first place and work as the second. The third place is somewhere people can go beyond their daily responsibilities—a space where they feel comfortable, safe, and part of a community.

This vision has gradually taken shape as MC² has grown. Beyond serving customers, the café now provides employment opportunities, including for working students, while continuing to build relationships with local producers and fellow business owners.

Looking ahead, Jim remains focused on strengthening his supply chain, improving processing capabilities, and preparing the business for sustainable growth.

“I don't want to expand without a clear supply chain,” he emphasized.

For Jim, success is not simply about opening more branches. It is about building a sustainable business that creates opportunities for his team, supports local coffee farmers, and continues to serve as a place where people can connect.

From a weekend coffee stand to a growing local business, MC² reflects how perseverance, customer trust, and meaningful partnerships can turn an aspiration into reality.

To those hoping to start their own business, Jim offers a message drawn from his experience:

“Don't wait, start already. You will figure it out along the way. Don't be afraid. Time is gold.”
