_From receiving educational assistance to becoming a cooperative staff member and youth officer, Jovie Balawag's journey reflects how continued support, trust, and meaningful involvement can help young people grow into leadership roles and contribute to the future of agriculture._

Growing up in a farming family, Jovie U. Balawag helped plant, fertilize, harvest, and sell produce while balancing her studies and other responsibilities.

Today, at 25, Jovie serves as an Agricultural Development Officer and youth treasurer of Alayon Multipurpose Cooperative in Barangay Pangasihan, Gingoog City, Misamis Oriental.

**Growing Up with the Cooperative**

Jovie's family has been part of Alayon since 2010. Her mother previously worked as its bookkeeper. Meanwhile, their family cultivated coconut intercropped with peanuts, watermelon, banana, and other fruit trees.

Jovie had to balance attending college, working part-time at a thrift store during the pandemic, and helping on their farm.

Through these experiences, she recognized agriculture as a potential source of sustainable livelihood, particularly when farmers have the knowledge to manage production and market their products.

In 2022, Jovie became one of ten students who received educational assistance from Alayon. This initiative helped children of cooperative members facing financial difficulties by covering transportation and other school-related expenses.

This became one of Jovie’s early experiences of how the cooperative supported its younger members. What she did not yet know was that her involvement with Alayon would eventually extend beyond receiving assistance to becoming part of its workforce and youth leadership.

**From Scholar to Cooperative Staff**

In 2024, Jovie graduated cum laude from the University of Science and Technology of Southern Philippines (USTP) in Claveria campus with a Bachelor of Science in Agriculture, major in Entrepreneurship.

Agriculture was not her first choice. She initially wanted to pursue culinary arts, but financial considerations and limited access to nearby schools led her to take a different path. Eventually, she discovered how her education in agriculture could help more her family's farm and prepare her for future business opportunities.

After graduation, Jovie volunteered at Alayon as a way of giving back to the cooperative. During this period, she attended a training conducted by the Department of Agriculture–Agricultural Training Institute (DA-ATI) on coconut nursery management, production, and processing.

Encouraged by the cooperative manager, she officially joined Alayon in August 2025 as an Agricultural Development Officer.

Her responsibilities include profiling coconut farmers, facilitating training, and assisting with project proposals. Through DA-ATI and the Department of Trade and Industry’s Rural Agro-enterprise Partnership for Inclusive Development (DTI-RAPID) training, she gained practical knowledge and confidence in performing her work.

Aside from the training, Alayon, with 720 members, was also assisted by the DTI-RAPID Growth Project under the Regional Coconut Detailed Investment Plan (DIP). It received a ₱1,057,536 matching grant for productive investments, including a copra dryer, hauling trucks, a drum kiln, and weighing scales, under a 60 percent project and 40 percent cooperative counterpart arrangement.

An additional ₱2,942,464 matching grant is being processed for organic fertilizer processing facilities and a vehicle for cooperative operations.

These developments connect with Jovie’s sharing of ideas gained from training. She shared the potential of producing fertilizer using coconut water and seaweed, an approach being studied by the Philippine Coconut Authority. She also shared ways to process discarded coconut water into useful products, such as vinegar, to address disposal concerns.

Through her experiences, she showed how training could provide practical ideas that may contribute to the cooperative's developing operations. She also applied lessons on fertilizer use and explored coconut-based food products at home.

**Encouraging More Young People to Participate**

Beyond her work with farmers, Jovie serves as treasurer of Alayon's youth organization, which began engaging young members in 2022 and was formally organized in 2025.

The organization now has 35 youth members, comprising 21 males and 14 females, aged 18 to 28, with around 30 percent out-of-school youth.

Jovie recalled how management encouraged younger members to consider working at Alayon, reminding them that someone would eventually need to continue the cooperative's operations.

To prepare them, Alayon supports approved youth proposals, involves young members in its activities, and connects them with institutions offering training and technical assistance.

Through partnerships with government agencies and other institutions, including DTI’s RAPID Growth Project, young members gain opportunities to develop entrepreneurial skills.

One example is the partnership with Xavier University's Service Learning Program, which supported a feasibility study on lettuce chips using coconut oil. The study contributed to a business proposal that placed second in DTI's Youth Entrepreneurship Program-Be Your Own Boss (YEP-BYOB) program.

Jovie found these opportunities to be meaningful for young people with limited access to education and training.

The cooperative also exposes young members to its services, businesses, and agricultural activities, helping them understand how these operate and identify opportunities they may pursue.

Seeing out-of-school youth gain knowledge, confidence, and practical skills is among the most fulfilling aspects of Jovie’s work. Having benefited from Alayon's support herself, she now helps other people experience the value of learning and participation.

**Preparing the Next Generation**

Jovie hopes to continue working at Alayon to strengthen her understanding of business operations and markets. She dreams of establishing her own business someday, possibly exploring healthier pastries that reflect her interest in culinary arts.

In agriculture, she also hopes to explore product innovations that could improve coconut utilization and create additional opportunities for farmers.

She believes the next generation can contribute to agriculture through technology, digital marketing, and new ideas. However, sustaining youth participation requires more than providing training and opportunities.

Although she observed that many young people are inclined to having immediate income, Jovie believes that practical exposure, continued guidance, and encouragement can gradually help them recognize the long-term opportunities in agriculture.

Her journey, from receiving educational assistance to becoming a cooperative staff member and youth officer, illustrates how an opportunity can become a starting point for continued learning, greater responsibility, and service to others.

She believes that even with opportunities available, young people must first recognize their value and be willing to learn.

**"If there is no mindset change, any intervention you give will not make a difference,"** she emphasized.

For Jovie, growing into leadership begins with this change in perspective—seeing opportunities not merely as assistance to receive, but as chances to learn, take responsibility, and eventually contribute to the growth of others.
