_With little experience in financial management, Grace Tananya took on a responsibility she initially doubted she could handle. Through the trust of her fellow officers and training supported by the RAPID Growth Project, she is helping strengthen her cooperative's financial practices while gaining the confidence to guide others._

When Grace M. Tañaña was asked to serve as credit committee cashier of La Libertad Farmers Multi-Purpose Cooperative (LALIFAMCO) in La Libertad, Kapatagan, Lanao del Norte, her first question was simple: Why me?

At 28, she was the youngest among the cooperative's officers, most of whom were already in their 40s and 50s. A high school graduate with no previous experience in lending or bookkeeping, Grace was unsure whether she could handle the responsibility.

But the older officers believed in her. They taught her that they, too, had started without knowing everything about their roles. What mattered was being willing to learn.

Five years later, Grace continues to serve the cooperative, applying the skills she has gained and helping improve its financial recording practices.

**Learning Through Trust and Guidance**

When Grace first became a cooperative officer, adjusting to her responsibilities was challenging. Aside from being the youngest, she had limited knowledge of lending procedures and financial recording.

Fortunately, the more experienced officers were willing to guide her. They patiently explained the processes and encouraged her to ask questions whenever she encountered difficulties.

This support became an important foundation for her development, particularly as LALIFAMCO began strengthening its financial management practices.

Through the TAMbayayong initiative, a collaboration between the Department of Trade and Industry's Rural Agro-enterprise for Partnership and Inclusive Development (RAPID) Growth Project and the Mindanao State University–Iligan Institute of Technology (MSU-IIT), Grace and her fellow officers received coaching and mentoring on accounting, taxation, and financial management.

The initiative helped farmer organizations improve their financial systems and develop the skills needed to manage their operations more effectively.

For LALIFAMCO, the assistance included hands-on coaching from an MSU-IIT intern who stayed in Kapatagan for three days and two nights to help the officers organize and establish their financial records.

There were also monthly coaching and mentoring sessions at the MSU-IIT campus from 2024 to present, focusing on bookkeeping and financial management, basic use of Microsoft offices, including the monitoring and assessment of their application of the knowledge and skills gained.

Grace recalled how challenging the sessions were, particularly because many of them had limited experience using computers.

At times, they had to request for repeated demonstrations to fully understand the procedures. Despite the difficulties, the MSU-IIT team patiently guided them through the process.

With a computer donated by a non-government organization and continuing spreadsheet training, the cooperative gradually began moving from manually searching physical ledgers toward more accessible digital financial records.

From previously preparing reports, requiring officers to go through record books, and locating transactions individually, information can now be retrieved more quickly with their developing system. This makes financial monitoring and report preparation easier.

Learning to use these tools was not simply about adopting technology. For Grace, it also meant becoming more capable of fulfilling her responsibilities and contributing to the cooperative's operations.

**Growing in Confidence, Sharing What She Learned**

Beyond improving her technical skills, Grace also noticed changes in herself.

When she first joined LALIFAMCO, she was hesitant to speak and often waited to be asked before sharing her thoughts. Working alongside experienced officers and participating in training gradually helped her become more confident.

Today, she takes a more active role in cooperative activities, represents the organization when needed, and contributes ideas on improving lending operations, including ways to follow up with members experiencing repayment difficulties.

Her learning has also extended beyond her own responsibilities.

When another cooperative member sought assistance with financial recording, Grace shared the basic bookkeeping practices she had learned from her fellow officers and through training.

From being a learner, she is now able to teach someone else. That was a meaningful sign of progress.

"I felt that I really had learned something I could share," she recalled.

What began as a responsibility had become an opportunity to develop her skills and help others.

**Preparing the Next Generation**

Grace believes that young people have an important role in sustaining farming communities and the cooperatives that support them.

"If there are no farmers, we have nothing to eat," she emphasized.

For Grace, education and technology should not necessarily lead young people away from farming. Instead, they should seek knowledge and skills that can help introduce better practices, improve productivity, and make agricultural work more manageable.

Through her own experience in LALIFAMCO, she encourages young people who are hesitant to accept leadership roles to seek guidance from those with more experience and remain open to learning.

At the same time, she recognizes the importance of experienced cooperative members who are willing to share their knowledge and give younger members the opportunity to grow.

For Grace, the cooperative's future depends not only on strengthening its systems and operations but also on preparing people who can continue its work.

From questioning why she was chosen as cashier to helping improve financial records and guiding fellow members, Grace's journey shows how trust, patience, and continued learning can develop confidence and responsibility.
