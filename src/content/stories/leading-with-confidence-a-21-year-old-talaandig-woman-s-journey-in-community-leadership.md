Genna Camanian was only 21 years old when she became president of the Paglambu Kaatuan Landcare Cluster (PLKC) in 2022. As a young woman from the Talaandig Indigenous community in Barangay Kaatuan, Lantapan, Bukidnon, she was entrusted with leading an association whose members had far more years of experience than she did.

Not everyone was convinced that someone her age could handle the responsibility.

“When I became president, I could not say that everyone was happy because they looked at my age,” Genna recalled.

For Genna, taking on the position meant stepping into unfamiliar responsibilities. She had to learn how to facilitate meetings, make decisions, represent the association in discussions with partners, and work with members who had years of experience.

But rather than allowing the doubts to discourage her, she chose to keep learning.

“I did not stop learning. I told myself that I could do it because I trusted my co-members. I wanted to help improve their livelihoods.”

With determination, guidance from experienced association members, and support from the Department of Trade and Industry's Rural Agro-enterprise Partnership for Inclusive Development and Growth (DTI-RAPID Growth) Project, Genna gradually developed the confidence and skills to lead.


**Rooted in Coffee and Community**

The Talaandig are among the Indigenous Peoples of Bukidnon, with cultural traditions reflecting a close relationship with the land. Agriculture has long been an important part of community life.

For Genna, this connection to the land is deeply personal. Coming from a family of coffee farmers, she grew up watching her grandparents and parents work on their family farm. At the age of 10, she began helping her grandmother harvest coffee, learning through observation and hands-on experience.

“I watched how they harvested coffee, and because I was curious, I tried it myself,” she shared.

During harvest season, Genna and her cousins would help gather coffee cherries from their family farm. What began as childhood curiosity gradually became an appreciation of the crop that had supported her family for generations.

As she grew older, she came to understand coffee's importance beyond the farm. The income her family earned helped provide for their daily needs and supported the education of Genna and her five siblings.

“It was not big,” she says, “but at least it helped them financially so we could access education.”

For Genna, coffee farming was more than a source of livelihood. It was part of her family's identity and one of the reasons she and her siblings had opportunities to pursue their education.

This connection to coffee would later become one of her motivations for serving fellow farmers in her community.
 
**Learning to Lead Despite the Doubts**

Established in 2008, PLKC is a workers' association based in Barangay Kaatuan, Lantapan, Bukidnon, with 141 members.

When Genna was given the opportunity to serve as president, she considered it a privilege, especially knowing that the association's pioneering members had placed their trust in her.

Yet accepting the position did not immediately make her confident.

“At first, it was not easy. I needed to build my confidence, especially in speaking and representing our association in discussions and agreements,” she recalled.

As president, she was responsible for facilitating meetings, coordinating with officers, assigning responsibilities, and helping members understand their roles in the association.

She also had to learn how to weigh different considerations before making decisions that could affect the members.

Genna credits much of her development to the guidance she received from experienced association members and the DTI-RAPID Bukidnon.

Through leadership training and continued interaction with project staff, Genna learned how to manage responsibilities, consider the advantages and disadvantages of decisions, and work more effectively with her fellow officers.

One experience that particularly changed her perspective was attending a Coffee Council meeting in 2022, where she joined cooperative and association leaders from different parts of Bukidnon.

Most of the participants were older and had extensive experience in managing their respective groups. Genna remembered being among the youngest in the room. But instead of feeling discouraged by the difference in age and experience, she saw an opportunity to learn.

“That was when I realized that age is not a barrier to becoming a leader,” she said.

She listened as experienced leaders shared how they started, managed challenges, and developed their groups. Their experiences offered practical lessons she could bring back to PLKC.

“I never imagined that I would have the opportunity to talk with people who had so much experience and expertise in managing an association or cooperatives,” she reflected.

Genna began to understand that leadership did not require knowing everything from the beginning. It also meant being willing to listen, seek guidance, and learn from others.
 
**Balancing Leadership, Education, and Family**

However, Genna's responsibilities extended beyond the association. She also had to balance her academic requirements with her leadership responsibilities.

There were occasions when her classes coincided with visits from project partners and other agencies. She recalled completing her classes before joining these engagements because she understood the importance of meeting the people supporting their community.

The support of her fellow officers, DTI-RAPID staff, and family helped her manage these responsibilities.

Her leadership experience also found its way into her education. As a Development Communication student at Bukidnon State University, she shared her experiences as a coffee farmer, an Indigenous youth, and an association leader with her classmates and teachers.

In 2026, she graduated cum laude with a Bachelor of Science in Development Communication, carrying with her the lessons she had gained both inside and outside the classroom.
 
**Turning Opportunities into Shared Learning**

As Genna became more confident in her role, she also recognized that the opportunities she received could benefit others.

Through the DTI-RAPID Growth Project, PLKC members and officers gained access to capacity-building activities, including leadership development and coffee rehabilitation training.

For an association that previously had limited opportunities to participate in government-supported training, these activities helped members become more involved.

“Before the project, we had limited training opportunities because there were not many government agencies supporting our association. When the project came, our officers and members became more active because they were given opportunities to learn,” she explained.

Genna made it a point to share what she learned with fellow members and young people in the community.

“After attending trainings, I shared what we learned with my co-members and fellow youth. I encouraged them to see the opportunities in coffee and to appreciate their parents as coffee farmers because they are the reason we became beneficiaries of this project.”

She also encouraged fellow youth to participate in focus group discussions and engagements with visiting project partners and government agencies, particularly during field visits in Cinchona. These included the visit of International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) Associate Vice-President Donal Brown in June 2025, as well as visits by oversight agencies, including the Department of Finance (DOF) and the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) in July 2026.

For Genna, these engagements offered young people valuable exposure to development partners and government representatives, helping them better understand how development projects support their community and the opportunities available in the coffee value chain.

Her efforts reflected a simple principle she had learned through her own experience: when someone is given an opportunity to learn, that knowledge can become more meaningful when shared with others.
 
**Seeing Change in the Community**

The changes Genna witnessed extended beyond her personal development as a leader.

Through the DTI-RAPID Growth Project, PLKC's Detailed Investment Plan received IFAD No-Objection on September 21, 2021. The association subsequently received capacity-building support and matching grants for coffee farm expansion, benefiting 100 members based on project records.

She recognized that these investments could help improve farmers' livelihoods as the coffee plants became productive and generated income. Another significant development she highlighted was the 3.4-kilometer farm-to-market road in Kaatuan.

For Genna, the road represented a practical improvement in the daily lives of farmers, families, and students.

She recalled how elders previously relied on horses to transport agricultural produce to market. Students also had to walk along difficult roads, often arriving at school with muddy clothes.

Now the improved road has made their journey more convenient.

These changes reinforced her belief that the project's benefits should be valued and sustained by the community.
 
**A Leader Beyond Her Term**

Genna served as PLKC president from 2022 to 2024. Although her term has ended, she continues to see herself as someone who can contribute to the association and her community.

She hopes that PLKC members will continue to value the support they have received and take responsibility for sustaining the improvements made possible through the project.

One of her aspirations is to see PLKC eventually develop from a workers' association into a cooperative, creating further opportunities for its members.

“I want our members to appreciate what the project has given us and take responsibility for it,” she shared.

Now a college graduate and beginning to gain professional experience, Genna hopes to apply the knowledge and discipline she developed through her years of service.

Looking back, she considers her leadership experience an important part of her personal growth. The young woman who once had to build her confidence in speaking and making decisions eventually found herself sharing experiences with other leaders, professionals, fellow students, and young people in her community.

For fellow young people, especially those in her Indigenous community, Genna leaves a message shaped by her own experience:

“Leadership is not defined by age. It is defined by your hard work and dedication to serve the people in your community. If you see a problem and you know you can help, you can do it.”
